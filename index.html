Brand foundations

What companies should have

Brand strategy
Who the company serves, what those people need, why they should choose it, and what the company can credibly promise.
Messaging and voice
A one-sentence description, a few core messages backed by evidence, and writing examples for different situations.
Visual identity
Logo variations, typography, colors, and direction for photography, illustration, or icons, with usable source files.
Design language
Rules for putting the identity to work across layouts, including hierarchy, spacing, density, imagery, color, and motion where it matters.
Templates and reusable assets
Editable starting points for recurring work. For software, a product design system can also cover UI components, interaction patterns, accessibility, and their implementation in code.
Guidelines and ownership
One place to find current assets, rules, and examples, plus a person responsible for updates and decisions.

How companies get there

  1. Discover. Review existing materials, talk to customers and the team, and understand what people use today to solve the same problem.
  2. Decide the positioning. Choose the audience, the problem the company wants to be known for solving, and the strongest reason to choose it. Capture those choices in a short brief.
  3. Develop the identity in real applications. Try messaging and visual directions on things the company will actually ship, such as a homepage, product screen, or sales deck.
  4. Turn what works into rules and tools. Document the patterns that hold up across those applications. Make templates and components for recurring work.
  5. Roll it out and maintain it. Update the most visible touchpoints first. Use feedback from customers and teammates to refine the guidelines.

What to work on first

Start with a one-page positioning brief for the company. It should explain who Hojil helps in Mongolia, what brings clients to Hojil, and why they would choose the company. A factual starting description is “Hojil is a company in Mongolia offering AI consulting, software development, and AI development.”

  1. Review real and prospective engagements. Identify who approached Hojil, what problem they brought, what they had already tried, and what made them consider working with Hojil. Use those examples to choose an initial audience and the problems to lead with.
  2. Define the three services in buyer terms. For each service, capture when someone should reach out, what Hojil delivers, and what a successful engagement looks like. Spell out where consulting ends and implementation begins in the kinds of engagements Hojil wants.
  3. Gather proof for each service. Look for projects, outcomes, or client feedback that can be shared. Use hojil-tts as an AI development example and describe its experimental status accurately. The playground runs locally, the model export is private, and listening results come from a small development set.
  4. Apply the brief to a company overview. Draft a homepage or one-page capabilities overview with a clear company description, the three services, examples of work, and a way to get in touch. Use it to make the first visual decisions.

A useful first milestone is a brief that names the priority buyer and their problem, explains the three services in plain language, and supports each claim with an example wherever one is available.

01

Brand strategy

Positioning

Hojil helps enterprises in Mongolia improve operational efficiency by building AI and software around the way their teams work.

Who we serve

Operations and technology leaders at enterprises in Mongolia. The first buyer to look for is a team with a recurring process that takes too much staff time because people have to move information between documents, messages, and existing systems.

What clients hire us to do

  • AI consulting maps the workflow, identifies where AI could save time, and defines how to measure the improvement.
  • Software development builds the application and connects it to the systems and data the team uses.
  • AI development scopes, builds, and tests AI for specific tasks. Potential pilot tasks include extracting information from documents, routing requests, or drafting case summaries. Compare handling time and rework before and after a pilot.

What we’ve delivered

  • Golomt Insights for Golomt Bank — website with a CMS.
  • Gangan for Urangan Corporation — event website with a CMS.
  • Gerelty Today — publishing website with a CMS.

These projects show Hojil can deliver software clients use to manage and publish content. Hojil’s AI development work includes the experimental Khalkha Mongolian text-to-speech project hojil-tts and planned open-source evaluations of how LLMs perform in Mongolian.

Why work with Hojil

Hojil has delivered software that lets client teams manage and publish their own content. We bring that focus on usable software to AI work by proposing a pilot around one task, testing it on examples from the team’s workflow, and agreeing up front on how to measure the result.

02

Messaging and voice

Company description

Hojil helps enterprises in Mongolia improve efficiency with AI and custom software. We can scope and test AI applications for tasks such as processing documents, routing requests, and finding internal information.

Homepage copy

AI and software solutions built around your operations

Hojil builds AI systems for enterprises in Mongolia. Start with a workflow such as extracting information from documents, preparing records for review, or routing requests across systems.

Call to action · Let’s find a solution

How we describe our services

AI development
We build and test AI for specific tasks. Possible applications include extracting information from documents, routing requests, and helping staff find internal information. We start with examples from your workflow.
AI consulting
We identify where AI could save staff time and define what the system needs to do and how to measure its performance.
Software development
We build custom applications and connect AI tools to the systems and data your team already uses.

What we can show

  • AI development. hojil-tts is an experimental Khalkha Mongolian text-to-speech project. Hojil also plans to publish open-source evaluations of how language models perform on tasks in Mongolian.
  • Client work. We built Golomt Insights for Golomt Bank, Gangan for Urangan Corporation, and Gerelty Today. All three are websites with content management systems. Use them as examples of software delivery and describe each project's scope as documented.

How we sound

  • Start with the work. Name what the team does and where time goes. Say “pull the key details from incoming applications” when that describes the task.
  • Be specific about results. Name what we can measure, such as time per request, processing errors, or how often staff have to redo a step. Quote improvements when we have measured them.
  • Use technical terms when they help the reader. An operations lead needs to know what staff will see and do. A technology lead may need to know which systems the tool will connect to and how data moves between them.

Examples by situation

First conversation

Which task takes your team the most time each week? We can walk through how it works today and see whether part of it is worth automating.

Request intake

Let's walk through what happens after a request arrives. Where do staff spend the most time collecting documents, checking information, or entering it into another system?

Pilot proposal

We'll measure how long staff spend processing a request today. During the pilot, the AI system will extract key details from incoming documents and prepare a draft record for staff to review. We'll compare handling time and how often staff need to correct the record.

Language for publishing

Proposed default for enterprise sales and marketing in Mongolia is Mongolian. Use English where partners, developers, or open-source contributors need it. Edit client-facing Mongolian copy against the intended meaning and how buyers talk about the work.

03

Visual identity

Wordmark

The Cyrillic lettering and blue asterisk are the starting point. Use the white lettering on black and the black lettering on light backgrounds. The square original works as a cover image.

Standalone asterisk

Use it where the full wordmark cannot fit, such as a favicon or small avatar.

Open symbol

Leave space around the wordmark equal to at least the width of the asterisk’s vertical stem. Both transparent wordmark files include this padding. Use a flat background and keep the proportions and colors as supplied.

Use these as starting minimums for screens. Open the original square SVG.

Color

Use black and white as the base. Use Hojil Blue for links on light surfaces, primary actions, and diagram highlights.

  • Black#000000
  • White#FEFEFE
  • Hojil Blue#0057FE
  • Light surface#F2F5FA
  • Secondary text#515B6B
  • Blue on black#8CB2FF

Use white text on Hojil Blue for filled buttons. Use the lighter blue for links and small text on black. These combinations give approximately 5.5:1 and 9.9:1 contrast respectively. W3C text contrast guidance

Typography

Use Inter for headings, body copy, and labels. It covers the Mongolian Cyrillic alphabet, including Ө, ө, Ү, and ү. Use 700 for headings, 600 for labels and emphasis, and 400 for body copy. Inter typeface

Design language

Use the same hierarchy across formats. Start with a clear headline, show the work or the evidence, and use blue to point to the next action or the step that matters.

Rules across all three

Hierarchy
Use bold Inter for the main message, medium weight for labels, and regular weight for explanations. Align headings, body copy, and evidence to the same left edge.
Spacing
Start from an 8 px grid. Use 8–16 px between related items, 24–32 px inside panels, and 48–64 px between major sections. Stack columns when the content needs more room.
Color
Use black for covers or hero sections and white or light gray for reading. Reserve blue for primary actions, links, and meaningful highlights.
Evidence
Use real screenshots, measured results, or simple diagrams. Label illustrative workflows and present client work according to the delivered scope.

06

Guidelines and ownership

Where to start

Use this guide for current brand wording and design rules. Start sales materials from the browser templates in section 05 and use the SVGs in section 03.

Who maintains it

Proposed roles for Hojil. One person can cover more than one role.

Company lead
Owns company positioning, service names, and visual identity.
Project lead
Confirms the project scope, results, and client material that can be shared.
Guide maintainer
Updates this guide and carries approved wording and design changes into the materials that use them.

How to keep it current

  1. Update the relevant section when a service, asset, or project description changes.
  2. Carry the change into the overview, deck, or templates where it appears.
  3. Reopen the updated materials in the browser and check the printed version before sharing.

For this review

  • Confirm that the AI development message describes the work Hojil wants to win.
  • Check the three delivered project descriptions and decide which examples to feature in sales materials.
  • Name the person who will keep the guide and templates current.
